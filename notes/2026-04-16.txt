1. Fundamentals of Ionization and Dissociation

Why Study This?
Real-world applications include neutralizing ant bites using baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) and understanding common substances like vinegar, soap, and salt.

Ionization vs Dissociation:

Ionization: Neutral molecules (like HCl) split into charged ions in a solution.
Dissociation: Ionic compounds (like NaCl) separate into ions that already exist when dissolved in water.
2. Understanding Acids

Definition:
An acid is a compound that releases hydronium ions (H₃O⁺) as the only positive ion in an aqueous solution.

Strong vs Weak Acids:

Strong acids: Ionize completely in water.
Weak acids: Do not fully ionize and exist in equilibrium.
3. Classification of Acids

Based on Source:

Organic Acids: Found in plants (e.g., acetic acid, formic acid).
Inorganic/Mineral Acids: Derived from minerals (e.g., HCl, H₂SO₄).

Based on Basicity:
(Number of H₃O⁺ ions released per molecule)

Monobasic: Releases one H₃O⁺ (e.g., HCl, HNO₃, acetic acid)
Dibasic: Releases two H₃O⁺ (e.g., H₂SO₄, H₂CO₃, oxalic acid)
Tribasic: Releases three H₃O⁺ (e.g., H₃PO₄)

4. Safety: Diluting Acids
Always add acid to water slowly while stirring.
Reason:
Dilution releases heat (exothermic reaction). Adding acid to water allows safe heat absorption, preventing splashing, boiling, or container damage.
